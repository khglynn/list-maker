# Podcast Recommendations — Mental Health & Brain Science

*Compiled 2026-05-31. Vetted against `../CLAUDE.md` (the reporting-backbone bar + format rules).*

**The brief:** well-produced, tight shows on the *science* of mental health — broad-spectrum
(bipolar, autism, ADHD, plus neuroscience/psychology/anxiety/trauma) — with a real
fact-checking and sourcing backbone. No shoot-from-the-hip, no rambly long-form interviews.

Every pick below cleared the **pass/fail gate**: documented fact-checking, cited studies, or
credentialed-researcher backing. Rejections (with receipts) are at the bottom — they're as
useful as the picks.

> **Confidence note (practicing what we preach):** only **Short Wave** and **Science Vs** have
> *explicitly named fact-checkers* in the evidence gathered. For others (Hidden Brain,
> Unexplainable, Radiolab), the backbone is "newsroom/science-desk standards + named expert
> guests" — strong, but not the same as a credited line-item fact-checker. Several official
> sites blocked direct fetches, so a few length/recency figures came from search snippets;
> flagged inline. Spot-check before treating any single number as gospel.

## ⭐ Start here (tight, active, highest rigor)

If you only try three, try these.

### 1. Short Wave (NPR) — *the tight daily explainer*
- **Why it fits:** The single best **named-fact-checker** signal of everything I vetted — NPR
  publicly credits fact-checkers by name (e.g., producer Rachel Carlson does "researching,
  sourcing, writing, fact-checking"; episodes credit fact-checkers like Tyler Jones). Backed by
  the NPR Science Desk.
- **Format/length:** ~**15 min**, daily. Exactly your "bite-sized, one idea, dense" slot.
- **Hosts:** Emily Kwong & Regina Barber (Barber has a physics background) — science journalists.
- **Start with:** the suicide-prevention / mental-health-care episode (w/ NPR health
  correspondent Rhitu Chatterjee); the eating-disorders-and-the-brain episode (w/ pediatrician
  Eva Trujillo).
- **Status:** Active. | Sources: npr.org/2022/10/12/1128149778 · npr.org/podcasts/510351

### 2. Science Vs (Spotify Studios) — *the gold-standard debunker*
- **Why it fits:** The show most synonymous with "cites the studies." Myth-busting built on
  reviewing the literature + interviewing the actual researchers, with **named fact-checkers**
  in the credits. This is the model your `CLAUDE.md` bar is written around. Took a public stand
  against platform health-misinformation in 2022.
- **Format/length:** ~**30–50 min** doc-style, weekly. (Spotify-exclusive — heads up.)
- **Host:** Wendy Zukerman, science journalist (biomed + law background).
- **Start with:** **"ADHD: Do We All Have It?"** (Apr 2025 — consulted Profs. Sibley,
  Sonuga-Barke, Shaw + others, fact-checked by Erica Akiko Howard & Marlowe Starling);
  **"Antidepressants"** (Profs. Kramer, Kirsch, Mayberg — note: older, ~2016).
- **Status:** Active. | Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Vs · apple ID 1051557000

### 3. Radiolab (WNYC) — *the RadioLab-tier brain documentary* (it literally is)
- **Why it fits:** Your stated production benchmark, and it's active. Named fact-checker credits
  (e.g., Diane A. Kelly); co-host Latif Nasser (PhD, History of Science, Harvard) describes the
  standard as "factual, carefully vetted"; full transcripts published. Senior producer Molly
  Webster runs a neuroscience sub-strand.
- **Format/length:** Sound-rich documentary, ~**36–50 min** (occasional 10–15 min shorts).
- **Start with:** **"The Ecstasy of an Open Brain"** (Nov 2024, 36 min) — neuroscientist Gül
  Dölen on psychedelics reopening neural "critical periods," with PTSD/stroke/brain-injury
  implications.
- **Status:** Active. | Sources: radiolab.org · radiolab.org/podcast/the-ecstasy-of-an-open-brain/transcript

## 🎧 The broad-spectrum core (active, on-topic)

### 4. Speaking of Psychology (American Psychological Association)
- **Why it fits:** Best **direct mental-health coverage** of the bunch, and the strongest
  institutional provenance — produced by the APA, every episode interviews a credentialed PhD
  researcher about their own peer-reviewed work. NYT ranked it #2 health podcast "firmly
  grounded in science." It's an interview show, but topic-focused and expert-anchored — not
  "let it roll."
- **Format/length:** ~**30–45 min**, weekly.
- **Start with:** "Why are more adults being diagnosed with ADHD?"; "ADHD among children and
  adults, with Margaret Sibley, PhD"; "How early detection could change autism diagnosis."
- **Status:** Active since 2013. | Sources: apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology

### 5. Hidden Brain (Shankar Vedantam)
- **Why it fits:** Behavioral-science narrative built around a research question answered with
  named academic guests; marketed on "scientific and journalistic rigor." Vedantam is a 25-year
  science journalist (ex-NPR, ex-WaPo, Harvard Nieman fellow). Caveat: narrative/interview style,
  no published line-item citations — rigor is science-desk-grade, not Science-Vs-explicit.
- **Format/length:** ~**40–60 min** (avg ~44), weekly. Toward the long end of your band but produced, not rambly.
- **Start with:** **"Rethinking Depression"** (reframes depression beyond the broken-brain model).
- **Status:** Active. | Sources: hiddenbrain.org/about · npr.org/series/423302056

### 6. The Science of Happiness (UC Berkeley GGSC / PRX)
- **Why it fits:** Hosted by an actual academic — **Dr. Dacher Keltner**, UC Berkeley psych
  professor and founder of the Greater Good Science Center. Each episode runs a research-tested
  practice, then scientists explain the mechanism. University-backed; great for mood/stress/
  resilience (less clinical on bipolar/ADHD/autism).
- **Format/length:** Tight, ~**16–20 min**, biweekly. (Companion "Happiness Break" = short guided practices.)
- **Status:** Active, 300+ episodes. | Sources: greatergood.berkeley.edu/podcasts

### 7. Unexplainable (Vox) — *for neuroscience curiosity*
- **Why it fits:** Vox science-desk reporting on the edge of the known; academic guests, full
  transcripts at vox.com/unxtranscripts. Leans neuroscience-mystery (the brain, perception,
  psychedelics) rather than clinical mood disorders.
- **Format/length:** ~**20–35 min**, twice weekly.
- **Start with:** the psychedelics-as-mental-health-treatment episode; the "dark side of
  meditation" episode.
- **Status:** Active. | Sources: apple ID 1554578197 · podnews.net/podcast/i7duf

## 🔬 Topic-specific, when you want to go deeper

### Autism
- **The Loudest Girl in the World (Pushkin)** — *the* autism documentary pick. Reporter Lauren
  Ober (This American Life / 99% Invisible / Criminal pedigree) on her own adult autism diagnosis
  at 42. Reported personal-documentary, **not** an interview show. **Finished limited series**
  (~15 eps, 2022) — a clean binge. Sources: pushkin.fm/podcasts/loudest-girl-in-the-world
- **Autism Science Foundation — Weekly Science Report** — researcher-hosted by **Dr. Alycia
  Halladay, PhD** (ASF Chief Science Officer); each ~**10–15 min** episode summarizes the week's
  newly published peer-reviewed autism research. Best-in-class science fidelity, zero fluff.
  Active weekly since 2015. *(Avoid ASF's separate "Adventures in Autism" — that one's personal
  stories.)* Sources: asfpodcast.org

### ADHD
- **Hyperfocus with Rae Jacobson (Understood.org)** — produced, Invisibilia-style narrative ADHD
  journalism from a credible neurodiversity nonprofit; brings on researchers/clinicians, tackles
  contested questions (microdosing, over-pathologizing). Active, biweekly (launched late 2024).
  Sources: understood.org/en/podcasts/hyperfocus
- For ADHD *science* specifically, **Science Vs's ADHD episode** (above) and **Speaking of
  Psychology's** ADHD episodes are the most rigorous entry points.

### Bipolar / mood
- **Inside Bipolar (Psych Central / Healthline)** — the cleanest bipolar-specific fit: co-hosted
  by **Dr. Nicole Washington (board-certified psychiatrist)** + Gabe Howard (lived experience),
  under a medical-publisher's editorial review. Tight, topic-driven episodes. Multiple 2024
  awards. Sources: psychcentral.com/blog/ibp
  - *Honest gap:* there is **no RadioLab-tier reported documentary series on bipolar (or ADHD)**
    in podcast form right now — those topics are currently better served by books/TV (e.g.,
    *Hidden Valley Road*). Worth remembering when we build the books list.

## 📚 Finished archives worth binging

- **Lost Patients (KUOW + The Seattle Times)** — the strongest *reported* mental-illness
  docuseries I found. 6-part investigation into why people with serious mental illness/psychosis
  cycle between streets, jail, and ER as psychiatric beds vanished. **Won 5 Regional Murrow
  Awards (2025)**, Peabody-nominated. ~30–40 min episodes. Newsroom-grade rigor.
  Sources: kuow.org/podcasts/lost-patients · npr.org/about-npr/1235778017
- **Invisibilia (NPR)** — the premier finished reported-psychology back-catalog (cancelled 2023).
  ~37 min, up to 4 months to produce each. Start: "The Secret History of Thoughts" (intrusive
  thoughts & identity); "Fearless." Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisibilia

## ⚖️ Borderline — good, but bumps your format rule

- **Ologies (Alie Ward)** — genuinely well-sourced (per-episode source links, credentialed
  single-expert guests; ADHD episode w/ Russell Barkley, plus Traumatology/OCD/Molecular
  Neurobiology). **But** the main feed is **60–100+ min long-form interview** — your hard-pass
  format. Workaround: the **"Smologies"** short, kid-friendly re-cuts. Use selectively.
- **Depresh Mode (John Moe, Maximum Fun)** — credible public-radio journalist, real expert guests
  (NAMI's Ken Duckworth, Surgeon General Murthy), well-produced. **But** Wikipedia describes it as
  "long-form interviews" — borderline on your dislike. Cherry-pick the expert/explainer episodes
  over the celebrity-memoir ones. (Successor to the now-finished *Hilarious World of Depression*.)

## ❌ Rejected (and why) — the taste-encoder

Popular, but they fail the backbone bar, the format rule, or both. Evidence included so future-me
doesn't re-litigate.

| Show | Why cut |
|------|---------|
| **No Such Thing As A Fish** | *Your own anti-pattern* — likable format, but not credulous enough and no sources. The reason this rubric exists. |
| **Huberman Lab** | Scientists document overstated/uncited claims (rat study described as "in people"); "vague" sourcing; multi-hour monologue/interview format. *Sources: Slate 2024-03; Rolling Stone.* |
| **The Telepathy Tapes** | ⚠️ Especially relevant given the autism interest: promotes "facilitated communication," debunked by APA/AAP/AACAP; tests were unblinded. ASAT says it "spreads misinformation." Documentary packaging ≠ rigor. *Sources: Wikipedia; The Skeptic; Vice.* |
| **The Diary of a CEO** | BBC World Service: of 23 health episodes, 15 averaged ~14 harmful claims contradicting evidence (anti-vax, "keto cures cancer," "autism reversible"). Long unstructured interview. *Source: BBC / pharmaphorum.* |
| **Maintenance Phase** | "Debunking" framing but documented numerical errors & cherry-picked studies; no fact-checker; hosts are journalist/author, not clinicians; weight-focused. *Sources: Wikipedia; Spurious Semicolon.* |
| **Mindscape (Sean Carroll)** | 1–3 hr "interview and let it roll" conversations, no fact-check apparatus. Host is rigorous; the *format* isn't what you want. |
| **Mel Robbins / Jay Shetty / School of Greatness / Feel Better Live More / We Can Do Hard Things / Mental Illness Happy Hour** | Coaching/celebrity-interview shows: unqualified or unvetted health advice, no fact-checking, long rambly format. (Shetty: Guardian/STAT credibility investigations; Feel Better platformed discredited anti-statin claims.) |
| **I Have ADHD / Bipolar Now** | Hosted by coaches, not clinicians/researchers; mindset framing over cited science. *(Bipolar Now also archived since 2022.)* |
| **Uniquely Human / Divergent Conversations** | Excellent autism credentials (Barry Prizant PhD; Megan Anna Neff PsyD) but conversational/long-interview format (35–77+ min); Divergent winding down. Make an exception only for a standout guest. |
| **Body Stuff (Dr. Jen Gunter)** | Rigorous and evidence-based, but a **finished** limited run and general-health (not mental-health) focus. Archive listen. |
